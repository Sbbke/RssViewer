**Main Headline:**
Having access to information does not guarantee ease of understanding.

**Sub-headline:**
While most people use the internet for health info, interpreting complex medical data remains a human challenge.

**Visual Layout:**
- A split-screen comparison or "Funnel" diagram.

### Slide 3: The Dermatology Challenge

**Main Headline:**
Users often lack the specific vocabulary to search effectively.

**Sub-headline:**
Without background knowledge, users struggle to move from "visual observation" to "precise terminology."

**Visual Layout:**
- A "Before vs. After" comparison layout.
- **Left (User Observation):** An image of a leg with red dots + Label: *"Red dots on legs"* (General).
- **Right (Medical Terminology):** The same image + Label: *"[Palpable purpura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palpable_purpura)"* (Specific).

**Annotations & Callouts:**
- **Key Insight:** AI bridges the gap between what we see and what experts name.

---

### Slide 4: Research Methodology: Evaluating Consumer Understanding

**Main Headline:**
Comparing how humans identify conditions with vs. without AI assistance.

**Sub-headline:**
A large-scale quantitative study using a three-arm randomized design to test naming accuracy and confidence.

**Visual Layout:**
- A horizontal process flow showing the 3 experimental arms:
  1. **Negative Control:** Standard Text Search (Familiar tools).
  2. **AI Arm:** Prototype AI Carousel (3–7 matching conditions + images/details).
  3. **Wizard of Oz (Positive Control):** "Perfect" predictions provided by a panel of dermatologists.

**Visual Components:**
- Flowchart with 3 distinct paths converging on the result: *User Understanding.*

---

### Slide 5: Quantitative Results: AI Boosts Naming Accuracy

**Main Headline:**
AI assistance significantly improves condition naming and user confidence.

**Sub-headline:**
Participants using AI were nearly 3x more accurate in identifying conditions compared to standard search tools.

**Visual Layout:**
- A grouped bar chart comparing "Condition Name Guessing Accuracy."
- **X-Axis Categories:** Control (8%), AI Arm (23%), Wizard of Oz (36%).

**Data / Figures:**
- **Willingness to guess:** 41% (Control) vs. 62% (AI).
- **Confidence Score:** Higher in the AI arm with significantly higher overall satisfaction and reduced search time.

---

### Slide 6: The Challenge of "Next Steps"

**Main Headline:**
Identifying a condition is easier than deciding on the next medical action.

**Sub-headline:**
While AI helps name what it sees, users still struggle to determine if they need a home remedy or an urgent clinic visit.

**Visual Layout:**
- A comparison table showing "Accuracy of Next Steps."

| Group | Accuracy % | Observation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Control** | 60% | Baseline for next steps. |
| **Wizard of Oz** | 63.5% | Slight improvement (statistically minor). |
| **Standard AI** | No significant change | Users often suggested less urgent actions than dermatologists. |

**Annotations & Callouts:**
- **Key Takeaway:** We need better tools to help users interpret "what to do next" rather than just "what it is."

---

### Slide 7: Human-Centered Research in Diverse Communities

**Main Headline:**
Real-world testing reveals how diverse communities interact with skin AI.

**Sub-headline:**
A mixed-methods study involving 110 participants across four primary languages to see how people use tools for their *own* concerns.

**Visual Layout:**
- A "Grid" layout showing screenshots of the research app in 4 different languages (Spanish, Chinese, etc.).
- Central icon representing a diverse community group and a clinician.

**Annotations & Callouts:**
- **Key Finding:** Participants relied heavily on visual pattern matching across various skin tones and severities.
- **Clinician Feedback:** 92% found the app helpful; 86% agreed with AI predictions.

---

### Slide 8: Conclusions & Future Directions

**Main Headline:**
The future of dermatology search is multimodal, personalized, and human-centered.

**Sub-headline:**
Combining images and text provides a superior experience for laypeople navigating complex medical information.

**Visual Layout:**
- A "Roadmap" or "Summary Checklist" layout with three pillars:
  1. **Textbook Examples:** Provide more visual variety to aid pattern matching.
  2. **Actionable Specificity:** Move from general condition info $\rightarrow$ specific user query actions.
  3. **Multimodal Approach:** Combine Image + Text for the best results.

**Visual Components:**
- Icons representing: [Image] + [Text] = [Better Understanding].
